Mobile World Congress 2024, Barcelona

26-29 February 2024, Fira de Barcelona Gran Via. I spent four days at MWC Barcelona, the biggest event in the world for mobile devices and the mobile communications industry, together with two colleagues from the Deloitte Digital mobile team.

Four days walking the halls of the Fira: keynotes and product launches, stands the size of a building, concept devices that will never ship, and a lot of hands-on time with hardware you normally only see in a video. Below is what stayed with me, with the photos and the videos I shot.

A trade show that moved four times

From a conference room in Brussels to the largest halls in Barcelona, in five moves.

  1. 1987 Brussels Pan Europe Digital Cellular Radio, the first edition
  2. 1990 Rome It becomes the GSM World Congress
  3. 1996 Cannes Ten consecutive years; 3GSM World Congress from 2003
  4. 2006 Barcelona, Fira Montjuïc The move to Spain
  5. 2013 Barcelona, Fira Gran Via Still its home today — where MWC24 was held

Android Avenue and the pin hunt

Android did not take a stand: it built an open-air avenue outside the halls, with its own robot statues, a café and a row of demo stations. The two features everyone queued for were Circle to Search — searching straight from whatever is on screen, without leaving the app — and Best Take on the Pixel 8, which picks everyone's best expression out of a burst of group photos. The Pixel Fold ran dual-screen Interpreter mode, showing the live translation on both sides of the phone so a conversation stays natural.

Google also ran a pin hunt across the fair: a map marking the stands of the participating manufacturers, a short demo of the newest hardware at each one, and an Android pin as a reward for every stop. Collect them all and you got a prize at the Android booth at the end of the day. Real-life gamification, and a very effective way to send thousands of people down a pre-determined route through the halls.

Gemini, in everything

The other half of Google's message was Gemini, spread across the whole ecosystem rather than announced as a single product: draft-and-plan inside Google Messages, Android Auto summarising incoming messages out loud so drivers keep their eyes on the road, Lookout describing images and text for low-vision users, Google Lens inside Maps, Wallet passes and transit directions on Wear OS. And the model itself in three sizes — Ultra, Pro and Nano, the on-device one — which is the part that ended up mattering most to me later on.

Xiaomi: Human × Car × Home

Xiaomi turned its stand into an ecosystem demo: the global launch of the Xiaomi 14 series, the smart-home wall, and in the middle of it all the SU7, the company's first electric car, with HyperOS as the layer meant to hold phone, car, tablet, watch and vacuum cleaner together. A phone maker showing a sedan on a turntable was, in one image, the whole point of MWC 2024.

Bendable, rollable, transparent

The concept devices were the most fun part of the fair. Motorola showed a bendable phone: a 6.9" pOLED panel on a woven fabric backplate with chains of tiny batteries, which wraps around your wrist and changes its interface depending on the shape you bend it into. Lenovo brought Project Crystal, the first laptop with a transparent 17.3" Micro-LED display — up to 55% transparency, meant for overlaying digital content on real objects. Samsung Display filled a room with panels that fold in both directions, wrap around a wrist ("Cling Band"), or roll out from phone size to tablet size. And the Galaxy Ring, still unreleased at the time, sat under glass in three colours and nine sizes.

Huawei and Honor

Huawei had the largest stand of the fair — a tunnel of LED panels leading into a domed arena — and used it for its 5.5G story: the first 5.5G intelligent core network, an award for its green antenna series, the Mate 60 and P60 flagships, and AI-based solutions for service providers. HONOR launched the Magic 6 series and the Magic V2 RSR Porsche Design foldable, with a robotic arm demoing the phone behind glass.

Robot dogs

Two of them, on opposite sides of the fair. Tecno unveiled Dynamic 1, an AI-powered robotic dog modelled on a German Shepherd — 8-core ARM CPU, a pack of sensors, stairs, and voice commands. Xiaomi brought the second generation of CyberDog. A small crowd stood around both of them all day.

XR and the AI Pin

VR, spatial computing and the metaverse were everywhere, but with a more practical tone than the year before: HTC VIVE showed shared VR experiences for teams and AI-driven translation inside virtual worlds, and most of the XR talk was about healthcare, architecture and training rather than social spaces. The device everyone wanted to try was the Humane AI Pin: no screen, voice and gestures only, a laser display projected onto your palm, real-time translation — $699 plus a subscription, and an idea of the post-smartphone interface that was worth queueing for even if you did not believe in it.

Deloitte at MWC

Deloitte was at the fair with a stand built around 5G, AI, Cloud, Cybersecurity, Sustainability & Climate and Operate services, with interactive demos rather than slides. The part I enjoyed most was the GenAI Makers Lab, where colleagues built and deployed new features with generative AI live, in front of whoever walked in — plus the panels on connectivity, the future of internet speeds and the scalability of 5G fixed wireless access.
