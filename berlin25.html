Fluttercon Europe + droidcon Berlin 2025

24-26 September 2025, CityCube Berlin. Three days at droidcon Berlin, the biggest Android event in Europe, and at Fluttercon Europe, the biggest Flutter and Dart gathering on the continent — two conferences held in the same building, on the same days, with a single badge that opens both.

I did not go only as an attendee: on the third morning I was on the droidcon stage, co-hosting the talk Inside Android: Context, Binder IPC, Zygote with my Deloitte Digital colleague Ioannis Anifantakis. Below is what those three days looked like — the talk, the two show floors, and the city in between.

From a room in Berlin to two conferences in one building

droidcon started in Berlin as a grassroots meet-up of Android developers, and sixteen editions later it is back in the same city — this time with a Flutter conference attached to it.

  1. 2009 Berlin The first droidcon, a couple of hundred developers
  2. 2022 London One of the largest editions so far, 1,400+ attendees
  3. 2023 Berlin The first Fluttercon, co-located with droidcon: 2,000+ together
  4. 2025 Berlin, CityCube 16th edition — where #dcbln25 and #ftcon25eu were held

Getting there

840 km from Via Santa Sofia 28 in Milano to Berlin, then an S-Bahn ride across the city every morning — the Fernsehturm showing up in the window somewhere around Alexanderplatz — and out at Messe Berlin, where the CityCube sits at the end of a long ramp.

Inside the CityCube: registration, and then pick a side

One badge, two conferences

The two events are co-located: one ticket registers you for both, and for three days the CityCube is split down the middle. On one side the Android tracks — Compose, Kotlin, architecture, AI on device, security. On the other the Flutter and Dart ones. Between the two, a shared expo floor, a shared lunch queue, and a lot of people wandering across the line because the interesting talk happened to be on the other side.

droidcon Berlin brought 1,300+ developers from more than 30 countries and 90+ talks and workshops across five tracks, with Google, JetBrains, Qualcomm, Gradle and Uber on the floor; Fluttercon added 1,000+ Flutter developers and 75+ tech talks, with keynotes from Kevin Moore ("Let's go far with Flutter") and Eric Seidel ("The Future is Written in Dart").

Inside Android: Context, Binder IPC, Zygote

Friday morning, droidcon stage. Inside Android: Context, Binder IPC, Zygote, co-hosted with Ioannis Anifantakis — a talk about the parts of Android nobody puts in a getting-started guide: what the Context actually is, how a process gets born, and why any of it matters when you are writing an app.

The first half is the Context as the real handle to the operating system, and the Manifest as the contract the system reads before your code ever runs. The second half is the one I took: Zygote, the process every Android app is forked from. Zygote boots once, loads the framework classes and the heap, and then every app launch is a fork of that already-warm process — a photocopier rather than a new machine — with copy-on-write memory shared between all of them. From Android 10 on, USAP (Unspecialized App Process) keeps a small pool of pre-forked, neutral processes waiting, so the fork does not even have to happen at tap time.

Fluttercon: Dash everywhere

The Flutter half of the building is impossible to mistake for anything else: a three-metre inflatable Dash in the middle of the floor, the same bird on every screen, and rooms full of people arguing about state management. The talks I sat through were the ones about pushing Flutter and Dart outside the phone: Jaspr, a web framework for Dart developers that renders real HTML and CSS instead of a canvas, and a deep dive on Flutter embedding — how to drop a Flutter view inside an app you did not write in Flutter.

Berlin

Then Berlin, which is a very hard city to visit casually. What is left of the Wall runs through the middle of it — 1.3 km of it painted at the East Side Gallery, a bare stretch behind the Topography of Terror, a line of cobbles across the asphalt where there is nothing left at all. Checkpoint Charlie is now a photo op between a KFC and a souvenir shop, with the original sign still telling you that you are leaving the American sector. And the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe is 2,711 concrete slabs a few hundred metres from the Brandenburg Gate: you walk in at street level and the ground drops away under you until the city disappears.

Urban Nation: street art, indoors

Where the paint does not stop at the Wall

Walk west along the Spree from the East Side Gallery and the painting simply carries on, off the Wall and onto whatever is standing. Holzmarkt is the clearest case: a stretch of riverbank rebuilt as a village of timber shacks, bars and clubs, every surface of it decorated. It looks nothing like the government quarter forty minutes away.

And, by pure luck, Oktoberfest

We came down from the tower and walked straight into it.
