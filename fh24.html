Flutter Heroes 2024, Torino

23 February 2024, Museo Nazionale dell'Automobile. One day at Flutter Heroes, the Italian conference dedicated to Flutter and Dart — third edition, in Torino and online, run by /synesthesia events — the same people who produce droidcon Italy, and who had put on React Native Heroes in this same building eight months earlier.

Out of Milano Centrale at 07.30, at the museum before nine, back on the 19.25 train home: a single day, one stage in the auditorium of a car museum, and a schedule that went from the weight an app leaves on a phone to accessibility, architecture and state management. Below is what that day looked like, with the photos and the clips I shot.

Three editions, and a car museum

Flutter Heroes started as the Italian stop of Google's Flutter Festival and then settled into the auditorium of the MAUTO, where it has been held ever since.

  1. 2022 Torino, Talent Garden The first edition, part of Google's Flutter Festival
  2. 2023 Torino, MAUTO Second edition, and the move to the car museum
  3. 2024 Torino, MAUTO Third edition, 23 February — where #FlutterHeroes24 was held

Getting there

140 km of train from Milano Centrale to Torino Porta Nuova and then the Museo Nazionale dell'Automobile on Corso Unità d'Italia — a museum of cars that for one day a year turns into a Flutter conference. The way in is a long dark corridor, with the conference's line-drawn bird on a sign at the bottom of it.

A full auditorium

Mind your app footprint

Three engineers of SNCF Connect & Tech — François Nollen, Alexandre Poichet and Alexis Choupault — took on the footprint an app leaves behind: size, network and battery, which stop being details the moment millions of people use what you ship. Smaller bundles with the Android App Bundle, tree shaking and deferred components, logic and SDKs moved off the client, and tuning for low-end devices, thin bandwidth and a nearly empty battery. Environmental impact as an engineering constraint, not a slide at the end.

"Mind your app footprint!", by the SNCF Connect & Tech team

Accessibility, from UI and UX

Another session made the case for accessibility in Flutter as a design decision before a coding one. Because Flutter draws every pixel itself, it is easy to build a single custom UI for both platforms — and just as easy to end up with components that no longer look or behave like anything the operating system offers, which is exactly where screen readers and system settings stop working properly. The advice: prefer UI and UX elements that stay close to native, and think twice before making one graphic language for Android and iOS.

"Accessibility starts from UI and UX"

Architecture, borrowed from Android

A whole session spent taking Android's recommended app architecture — UI layer, domain layer, data layer, each component only allowed to use the one below it — and putting it to work in a Flutter codebase: repositories as plain classes, data sources behind them, DTOs mapped into domain models at the boundary, and GetIt doing the dependency injection with a @singleton annotation — the same layering an Android codebase would use, on the other framework.

Bloc vs Riverpod

Nicolò Sordoni took on the argument that never ends in a Flutter team: Bloc or Riverpod, differences and similarities between the two most popular state managers, and what you actually gain and lose by picking one. The rest of the schedule ran just as wide: Craig Labenz from Google on the life cycle of a RenderObject, Dart FFI, security in Flutter apps, debugging performance with the DevTools, animations versus delivery — plus two hands-on workshops in the side rooms, one on navigation and one on end-to-end testing with Patrol.

"Flutter state management: Bloc vs Riverpod"

Dash, in the swag bag

And then the reason half the room queued at the desk: a plush Dash, the Flutter and Dart mascot, with its own little Dart tag around the neck. Two of them came home with us, one per developer, and spent the rest of the afternoon being photographed in front of the screen instead of the slides.

Home on the 19.25

One day, ten talks, a notebook full of things to try on Monday morning and a plush bird in the backpack — the 19.25 out of Torino Porta Nuova and back in Milano by half past eight. Three days later I was in Barcelona for MWC 2024, which is a different kind of week entirely. I went back to Flutter Heroes the year after, for the 2025 edition — in a cinema, that time.
